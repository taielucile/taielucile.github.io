Methods

How I work

I currently work with administrative records, survey data, and policy documents, usually in R. I check how records were produced, what their categories mean, and which conclusions they can support. I want to develop archival and historical research skills to study how professional expertise and diagnostic standards changed over time.

Reading the documentation

Understanding the records

Program membership is a count made under a school system’s eligibility and reporting rules. I read the documentation to understand that process, including changes in eligibility rules, category definitions, and the treatment of small counts.

  • What exactly is being counted?
  • Who is missing?
  • Did the definition change?
  • What was suppressed or combined?
  • What can I actually say from this?

Methods I use now

Complex surveys

National Survey of Children’s Health

For Who Gets Help First?, I combine two years of survey responses and account for the sampling design when estimating differences between groups. I also use the survey’s multiply imputed poverty measures so that missing income information is handled within the analysis. Regression models and adjusted probabilities help me compare service timing while accounting for the other characteristics included in the models.

Administrative records

Florida special-education data

The Florida study required combining 16 workbooks into a consistent dataset, then checking that district, year, and racial-group records matched correctly. I calculate program-membership rates and risk ratios and examine how they change under alternative enrollment denominators, district-size cutoffs, and assumptions about suppressed cells. I check whether the direction and size of a comparison change when I alter those choices.

Policy evaluation

Medicaid expansion and special education

In The Staying Power of Special Education, I use state-by-year data and a staggered difference-in-differences design to examine changes in IDEA identification following Medicaid expansion. I compare identification rates across states that expanded Medicaid at different times. A causal interpretation requires credible comparisons of how those states would have changed without expansion.

Policy research

Speech and language reporting

For the federal reporting project, I compare the categories named in state documents with those used in federal reporting. I retain the original definitions and track the source for each comparison. If the documents do not explain how two categories correspond, I mark the relationship as uncertain rather than assume they mean the same thing.

UsedCross-state comparisonPolicy documentsCategory mappingSource tracking

Reproducibility

Source files and analysis code

I keep the source files and write the cleaning and analysis steps in code, so I can follow a result back to the records and decisions that produced it. Git lets me track changes as the work develops. Checks for duplicate records, incorrect merges, impossible values, and missing data are part of that process, along with rerunning analyses under reasonable alternative definitions.

What I want to learn next

Further training

I want to study the history behind the classifications I use and how Black people describe their experiences of assessment and therapy. My next priorities are historical and archival methods, alongside further training in longitudinal analysis.

Quantitative

Following students over time

I want to learn how longitudinal and multilevel models, event-history and sequence analysis, and record linkage can help me study changes in young people’s lives. Further training in causal inference would strengthen my policy research.

Historical

Professional archives and diagnostic standards

Archival research and historical sociology would let me investigate how professional definitions of disability and school policies developed, including whose knowledge shaped them.

Qualitative

Accounts of assessment and therapy

I’m interested in Black children’s experiences of therapy and adults’ memories of childhood assessment and treatment. I want to learn how to study these accounts carefully, including questions about language identity and code-switching.

Critical and theoretical

How I frame the problem

I’m continuing to read Black feminist thought, Critical Race Theory, Critical Disability Studies, DisCrit, and Black disability scholarship. These traditions inform how I ask questions about normality, authority, racism, and ableism.
