How I work

Most of my current work starts with school records, survey data, or policy documents. I use R for the analysis, with code for cleaning the data and producing tables and figures. Much of the work happens before fitting a model, when I’m checking what a count means or whether two files can be compared.

National Survey of Children’s Health

My developmental-services study pools the 2023 and 2024 National Survey of Children’s Health, with an analytic sample of 13,930 children ages 6–17 who have service histories. I account for the survey design and all six multiply imputed poverty values, reporting adjusted odds ratios and standardized probability differences.

I distinguish service receipt from initiation timing and test condition-adjusted models, age-restricted samples, alternative age thresholds, and missing-data bounds.

Florida special-education data

The Florida dataset contains 1,608 analytic records constructed and audited from 16 workbooks. I check district, year, and racial-group identifiers before joining the records. The thesis design compares membership rates and risk ratios, with checks using different enrollment denominators and district-size cutoffs.

Medicaid expansion and special education

The Staying Power of Special Education links a 44-state panel from 2013–2023 to school Medicaid expansion timing, IDEA Part B counts, and Census population estimates for youth ages 6–21. I use Callaway–Sant’Anna and Sun–Abraham difference-in-differences estimators, with event-study, robustness, and disability-category analyses. The causal question depends on whether the comparison states provide a credible estimate of what would have happened without expansion.

Speech and language reporting

I compare state and federal definitions of speech and language impairment. Each comparison retains the source and original wording. Where the documents do not establish how categories correspond, I leave that correspondence unresolved.

California school meals and attendance

With Andrew Penner’s team at UC Irvine, I build R pipelines combining 2024–25 California Department of Education enrollment, absenteeism, and school data. I compare public measures with Irvine and Lodi district records, evaluate transitional-kindergarten and kindergarten attendance denominators, and produce school-level comparison files and technical memos.

Source files and analysis code

I preserve the source files and use Git to track code changes. Checks cover duplicates, joins, missing values, and impossible counts. Tables and figures are generated from the analysis code.
