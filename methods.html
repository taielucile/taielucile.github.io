Methods

How I work

My current work uses school records, survey data, and policy documents. I analyze the data in R and keep the cleaning, checks, tables, and figures in code. The studies below explain the choices involved.

Reading the documentation

Understanding the records

Florida’s program counts follow state eligibility and reporting rules. Before comparing rates, I check the definitions, enrollment denominators, and rules for suppressing small counts.

  • What exactly is being counted?
  • Who is missing?
  • Did the definition change?
  • What was suppressed or combined?
  • What can I actually say from this?

Methods I use now

Complex surveys

National Survey of Children’s Health

For Who Gets Help First?, I pool the 2023 and 2024 National Survey of Children’s Health. I use the survey weights and multiply imputed poverty measures, then fit regression models and estimate adjusted probabilities of service entry before age three.

Administrative records

Florida special-education data

The Florida dataset combines 16 workbooks. I check district, year, and racial-group identifiers before joining the records. The thesis design compares membership rates and risk ratios, with checks using different enrollment denominators, district-size cutoffs, and values for suppressed counts.

Policy evaluation

Medicaid expansion and special education

The Staying Power of Special Education uses state-by-year data and staggered difference-in-differences to examine IDEA identification after Medicaid expansion. The causal question depends on whether the comparison states provide a credible estimate of what would have happened without expansion.

Policy research

Speech and language reporting

I compare state and federal definitions of speech and language impairment. Each comparison retains the source and original wording. Where the documents do not establish how categories correspond, I leave that correspondence unresolved.

Reproducibility

Source files and analysis code

I preserve the source files and use Git to track code changes. Checks cover duplicates, joins, missing values, and impossible counts. Tables and figures are generated from the analysis code.

What I want to learn next

Further training

My next methodological priorities are archival research and longitudinal analysis. I’m also interested in retrospective interviews about childhood assessment and therapy.

Quantitative

Following students over time

Longitudinal data would let me examine the sequence of identification, services, and educational transitions for the same person. Florida’s annual program counts cannot show those individual paths.

Historical

Professional archives and diagnostic standards

Professional archives could help me trace changes in diagnostic definitions, assessment practices, and accounts of racial and linguistic difference within speech-language pathology.

Qualitative

Accounts of assessment and therapy

I’m interested in what Black adults remember about childhood assessment and therapy, including what they were told about their speech and what they were asked to change. Interviews would address remembered experience, with the limits that retrospective accounts entail.

Critical and theoretical

How I frame the problem

My theoretical questions concern how racism enters professional knowledge and how disabled people understand their own needs. Black feminist theory, Critical Race Theory, and Critical Disability Studies guide my reading of those questions.
