Research

My interests span education, race and racialization, disability, social stratification, organizations, and social policy. I examine how organizational practices, professional judgments, and public policies affect access to resources and services, and how individuals and communities navigate and reshape those institutions.

When Speech and Language Diverge

Honors Undergraduate Thesis · University of Central Florida

Racial and Ethnic Patterns in Florida Speech and Language Impairment Program Membership, 2017–2025

Do Black, Hispanic, and White students show different patterns of membership in Speech Impairment and Language Impairment programs? Florida’s separate records let me investigate differences that would be harder to see in the combined federal category.

These records describe program membership, rather than underlying need or individual diagnostic decisions.

Data, design, and limits

Design

The thesis uses 1,608 analytic records constructed and audited from 16 Florida Department of Education workbooks, covering 2017–18 through 2024–25. Analyses compare statewide and district membership patterns, rates, risk ratios, and denominator sensitivity. These are repeated cross-sectional records, so they do not follow individual students over time.

These records measure program membership under Florida’s reporting rules. A difference between groups would not, on its own, establish discrimination or tell me whether individual students were correctly identified. The records also contain no account of the therapy students received or how they experienced identification.

In progress. Defense planned for Spring 2027.

Racial and Socioeconomic Differences in Reported Developmental-Service Initiation Before Age Three

Sole-authored manuscript · Under review at Maternal and Child Health Journal

I examine reported developmental-service initiation before age three using the pooled 2023–2024 National Survey of Children’s Health. The analytic sample includes 13,930 children ages 6–17 with service histories.

The analysis separates service receipt from initiation timing. Black–White differences were clearer in when services began than in whether services were received. The timing analysis concerns children with service histories; it does not estimate unmet need among children who never received services.

Analysis and sensitivity checks

I account for the complex survey design and all six multiply imputed poverty values, reporting adjusted odds ratios and standardized probability differences. Sensitivity checks include condition-adjusted models, age-restricted samples, alternative age thresholds, and missing-data bounds.

Submitted to Maternal and Child Health Journal in November 2026.

What Federal Special-Education Data Hide

A Policy Case for Disaggregating Speech and Language Impairment

This paper compares state definitions of speech and language impairment with the combined category used in federal reporting. I record which distinctions disappear in the federal data and where the documents leave the correspondence between categories unclear.

Collaborative research

Since September 2026, I’ve worked with Andrew Penner’s team at UC Irvine on kindergarten meal access and attendance. I build reproducible R pipelines, compare public-data measures with district records, and assess attendance denominators.

With L. Trenton Marsh’s H.O.P.E. Lab, I support community-engaged research and school-based mentoring with middle-school-aged boys in a Title I school. I worked with the team in 2024–2025 and returned in Fall 2026.
