Research

Race, disability, and diagnostic authority

My current projects examine disability classification, developmental-service timing, and special-education reporting. I use school records, survey data, and policy documents. I’m also developing historical questions about speech-language pathology’s diagnostic authority and how white supremacy and anti-Blackness shaped its standards.

Research agenda

My current projects use school records, survey data, and reporting documents. The historical and qualitative questions below describe the research I want to pursue next.

01How did speech-language pathology establish its diagnostic authority? How did white supremacy and anti-Blackness enter the profession’s definitions, training, and assessment standards?

02How do clinicians assess communication disorders in children who speak African American English? How do school eligibility rules affect which children receive services and what treatment addresses?

03What do Black disabled young people want from speech and language services? How do their priorities compare with the goals set by clinicians and schools?

04What do Black adults remember about childhood therapy? How do they describe its effects on their speech, code-switching, and relationship to their language?

TheoryI read Black feminist theory and Critical Race Theory to think about how racism becomes part of professional knowledge. Critical Disability Studies and the social model of disability help me ask how services can address impairment while also imposing expectations about normal speech and behavior. Differences in program membership alone cannot settle these questions.

Honors Undergraduate Thesis · University of Central Florida

When Speech and Language Diverge

Racial and Ethnic Patterns in Florida Speech and Language Impairment Program Membership, 2017–2025

Data

The design uses 16 Florida Department of Education workbooks for 2017–18 through 2024–25, covering all 67 county school districts. These are annual counts, not individual students’ records.

Enrollment denominators and small counts

The primary denominator is same-race PK–12 enrollment. Planned checks use K–12 enrollment, different district-size cutoffs, and alternative values for enrollment counts suppressed below 10. Suppressed counts are treated as 1–9, with 5 as the midpoint.

Planned analysis

I will compare membership rates, risk differences, and risk ratios for Black and Hispanic/Latino students with those for White students, separately for Speech Impairment and Language Impairment. District and year comparisons will examine variation and temporal stability.

Question

Do Black, Hispanic, and White students show different patterns of membership in Speech Impairment and Language Impairment programs? Florida’s separate records let me investigate differences that would be harder to see in the combined federal category.

Design

The thesis design compares Speech Impairment and Language Impairment membership using Florida Department of Education records from 2017–18 through 2024–25. Planned analyses include rates, risk differences, risk ratios, district comparisons, temporal stability, and sensitivity checks. These are repeated cross-sectional records, so they do not follow individual students over time.

Data
Florida Department of Education administrative workbooks

Scale
16 workbooks · 1,608 district-year-race rows

Methods
R · program-membership rates · risk ratios · district and year comparisons · sensitivity checks

Status
Proposal under review as of October 2026. Defense planned for Spring 2027.

These records measure program membership under Florida’s reporting rules. A difference between groups would not, on its own, establish discrimination or tell me whether individual students were correctly identified. The records also contain no account of the therapy students received or how they experienced identification.

Who Gets Help First?

Racial and Socioeconomic Differences in the Timing of Developmental Service Entry Among U.S. Children

Study at a glance

Data
2023–2024 National Survey of Children’s Health
Sample
Children who received developmental services
Analysis
Survey weights · regression · adjusted probabilities
Missing data
Multiply imputed household-poverty measures

Among children who have received developmental services, who began before age three? I examine differences by race, ethnicity, and household poverty using the 2023 and 2024 National Survey of Children’s Health. The analysis accounts for survey weights and imputed poverty measures. It concerns timing among service recipients; it cannot estimate unmet need among children who never received services.
