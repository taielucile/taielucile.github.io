Research interests

Sociology of education; race and racialization; disability; social stratification; organizations and institutions; life course; social policy. My research examines how institutions shape inequality, opportunity, and life-course trajectories, with a focus on race, disability, and socioeconomic disadvantage. I am interested in how organizational practices, professional judgments, and public policies influence access to resources and services, and how individuals and communities navigate and reshape institutional systems.

Independent research

Researcher, Honors Undergraduate Thesis

Sep. 2024–Present

University of Central Florida

Chair: Jacqueline Towson, Ph.D. | In progress; planned defense Spring 2027.

When Speech and Language Diverge: Racial and Ethnic Patterns in Florida Speech and Language Impairment Program Membership, 2017–2025

  • Examine racial and ethnic patterns in speech and language impairment program membership using eight years of Florida Department of Education data across all 67 school districts.
  • Analyze Speech Impairment and Language Impairment separately for Black, Hispanic, and White students to examine patterns concealed by combined reporting.
  • Construct and audit 1,608 analytic records from 16 workbooks; examine statewide and district patterns, risk ratios, and denominator sensitivity.

Policy companion: What Federal Special-Education Data Hide: A Policy Case for Disaggregating Speech and Language Impairment

  • Examine how federal reporting under a combined Speech or Language Impairment category can conceal differences in racial and ethnic patterns of program membership.

Principal Investigator

Aug. 2026–Present

University of Central Florida

Racial and Socioeconomic Differences in Reported Developmental-Service Initiation Before Age Three

  • Designed and directed an independent, sole-authored quantitative study using pooled 2023–2024 National Survey of Children’s Health data (N = 13,930 children ages 6–17 with service histories). The study examines racial/ethnic and socioeconomic differences in whether developmental services began before age three.
  • Applied the NSCH complex survey design and all six multiply imputed poverty values. Reported adjusted odds ratios and standardized probability differences.
  • Separated service receipt from initiation timing, finding that Black–White differences were clearer in when services began than in whether they were received.
  • Tested robustness with condition-adjusted models, age-restricted samples, alternative age thresholds and missing-data bounds. Manuscript submitted to Maternal and Child Health Journal (Nov. 2026).

Principal Investigator

2026

University of Central Florida

The Staying Power of Special Education: What School Medicaid Expansion Reveals About Disability Classification

  • Built a 44-state panel (2013–2023) linking school Medicaid expansion timing after the 2014 Free Care Rule reversal to IDEA Part B child counts and Census population estimates for youth ages 6–21.
  • Coded state-by-state treatment timing from policy chronologies and implementation documents, with a documented source trail for each assignment.
  • Estimated staggered difference-in-differences models (Callaway–Sant’Anna; Sun–Abraham) of school Medicaid expansion’s effect on IDEA identification, including event-study, robustness, and disability-category analyses.
  • Sole-authored manuscript on the persistence of special-education classification under school Medicaid expansion; currently under review at Educational Evaluation and Policy Analysis.
