I’m an undergraduate sociologist at the University of Central Florida. I was born in Jamaica and grew up between Jamaica and the United States.

Education

B.S. Sociology, in progress
University of Central Florida

Minors in Cultural Anthropology
and Africana Studies

Before college, I worked in healthcare, where I helped uninsured patients get referrals and medication and arrange follow-up care after ER visits. When I started college, I wanted to become a speech-language pathologist and work with Black and Latino children in schools with limited resources.

While studying special education, I kept wondering how clinicians learned to tell a language difference from a disorder. I wanted to know where those distinctions came from, why schools relied on them, and how racism shaped the way adults heard Black children speak. Those questions led me to sociology.

I’m interested in how race, disability, and class shape the decisions schools and other institutions make about young people, and what those decisions mean as they get older. For my thesis, I’m comparing enrollment in Florida’s Speech Impairment and Language Impairment programs by race and ethnicity. I also have two papers under review as sole author. One looks at when children first receive developmental services. The other examines how Medicaid expansion relates to special education identification.

I work with Andrew Penner’s team at UC Irvine on school meal access and attendance. At UCF, I mentor middle schoolers and help with research through H.O.P.E. I’m also part of a team comparing Hope Card laws across the U.S.

I love to dance, I’m into interior design, and I watch RuPaul’s Drag Race. I’m vegan and love trying dishes from different cultures.
