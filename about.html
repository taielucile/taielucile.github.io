Taiel Lucile

they/them/él

I’m an undergraduate sociologist at the University of Central Florida. I was born in Jamaica and grew up between Jamaica and the United States.

Before college, I worked at Brevard Health Alliance, helping uninsured patients arrange referrals, obtain medication, and follow up after emergency-department visits. I entered college hoping to become a school-based speech-language pathologist working with Black and Latino children from low-resource communities.

Studying special education made me question how the profession had learned to distinguish communication disorders from language differences. I wanted to understand where its definitions came from, why schools accepted them, and how racism shaped judgments about Black children’s speech. Those questions are what drew me to sociology.

My interests now span education, race and racialization, disability, organizations, social stratification, and the life course. My thesis compares racial and ethnic patterns in Florida’s Speech Impairment and Language Impairment programs. I also have sole-authored manuscripts under review on developmental-service initiation and school Medicaid expansion.

Alongside my own projects, I work with Andrew Penner’s team at UC Irvine on school meal access and attendance. At UCF, I contribute to H.O.P.E.’s school-based research and mentoring and to comparative research on Hope Card laws.
