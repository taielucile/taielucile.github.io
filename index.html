I study race, disability, and institutions

I’m an undergraduate sociologist at the University of Central Florida. I study disability classification and access to services for Black children and young people from low-resource communities. My historical interests concern speech-language pathology’s diagnostic authority and how anti-Blackness shaped the profession.

Learn more about my work

When Speech and Language Diverge

Racial and ethnic patterns in Florida speech and language impairment program membership, 2017–2025

Florida records Speech Impairment and Language Impairment as separate categories, but federal reporting combines them. Using eight years of records from all 67 Florida school districts, I examine whether combining the categories obscures differences in program membership among Black, Hispanic, and White students.

A little about me

I was born in Jamaica and grew up between Jamaica and the United States. I’m now studying sociology at UCF, with minors in Cultural Anthropology and Africana Studies. Before sociology, I planned to become a school-based speech-language pathologist.
