I study race, disability, and institutions.

I’m a sociologist studying racialized diagnostic authority: how professions establish the standards used to identify disability, and how those standards govern access to services. My interests center on speech-language pathology, anti-Blackness, and the lives of Black disabled children and youth from low-resource communities.

What I keep coming back to

How does a profession become an authority on disability?

My historical questions concern how speech-language pathology established its authority to distinguish communication disorders from language differences. I want to investigate how white supremacy and anti-Blackness shaped the knowledge, assessment standards, and professional institutions through which those distinctions became credible.

That agenda also concerns what diagnosis does in children’s lives. I’m interested in the relationship between obtaining needed services and being expected to change one’s speech, including African American English, to meet school and clinical standards. I want to examine how Black children understand those encounters and how adults remember their consequences for language identity and self-determination.

When Speech and Language Diverge

Racial and ethnic patterns in Florida speech and language impairment program membership, 2017–2025

Federal reportingSpeech or Language ImpairmentSLI

Florida records Speech Impairment and Language Impairment as separate categories, but federal reporting combines them. Using eight years of records from all 67 Florida school districts, I examine whether combining the categories obscures differences in program membership among Black, Hispanic, and White students.

How I came to sociology

From speech-language pathology to sociology

I entered college planning to become a school-based speech-language pathologist working with Black and Latino children from low-resource communities.

My questions moved from clinical practice to the history and sociology of the profession: how its knowledge became authoritative, how race entered its standards, and what those standards mean for the children being assessed.
