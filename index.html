Questions behind the work

How did these definitions become accepted?

I want to trace how speech-language pathology established its definitions of disordered communication, including how white supremacy shaped the profession’s training and assessment practices. My current statistical studies address a narrower question about which children appear in school disability categories.

I also wonder what Black children make of what they’re asked to change in therapy. The distinction between a disorder and African American English matters here. Adults’ memories of childhood therapy could offer another way into the question, including how those experiences affected their relationship to their speech.

When Speech and Language Diverge

Racial and ethnic patterns in Florida speech and language impairment program membership, 2017–2025

Federal reportingSpeech or Language ImpairmentSLI

Florida records Speech Impairment and Language Impairment as separate categories, but federal reporting combines them. Using eight years of records from all 67 Florida school districts, I examine whether combining the categories obscures differences in program membership among Black, Hispanic, and White students.

How I came to sociology

From speech-language pathology to sociology

I entered college planning to become a school-based speech-language pathologist. As I studied special education, I kept wondering where its definitions came from and why schools accepted them. I wanted to understand how racism shaped judgments about Black children’s speech. That took me toward the history and sociology of the profession.
